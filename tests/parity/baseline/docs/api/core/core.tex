\section{Diagnostics emitters}\label{diagnostics-emitters}

\texttt{texsmith.core.diagnostics} defines the \texttt{DiagnosticEmitter} protocol plus a few
stock implementations. Pass any emitter into \texttt{ConversionService},
\texttt{convert\_documents}, or \texttt{TemplateSession} to intercept warnings, errors, and
structured events.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
\textbf{Emitter} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Typical usage} \\

\midrule
\texttt{CliEmitter} (\texttt{texsmith.ui.cli.diagnostics}) & Rich-powered emitter used by the Typer CLI. Respects \texttt{-\allowbreak{}v} and \texttt{-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}debug}, paints warnings as panels, and streams structured events to the diagnostics sidebar. & Default when running \texttt{texsmith}. Import it in automation scripts when you want human-friendly output. \\
\texttt{LoggingEmitter} & Forwards \texttt{warning}, \texttt{error}, and \texttt{event} calls to the standard \texttt{logging} module. & Daemons, notebooks, or services that rely on existing logging policy. \\
\texttt{NullEmitter} & No-op implementation. Useful when you want silent conversions or plan to capture diagnostics out-of-band. & Unit tests and benchmarking. \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Emitters expose a \texttt{debug\_enabled} flag so downstream handlers can decide whether
to include stack traces or expensive state dumps. Implement your own to route
diagnostics to metrics systems or structured loggers.
