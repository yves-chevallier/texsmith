Like vanilla Markdown, you can apply basic text formatting using a variety of
delimiters. Every inline node has a \textbf{role} as its canonical spelling, and most
have a familiar shorthand as sugar; both produce the same node.
\begin{center}

\toprule

\midrule
Emphasis & \tscodeinline{*x*} & \tscodeinline{\_x\_} & \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}emph} \\
Strong & \tscodeinline{**x**} & (none) & \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}textbf} \\

\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{g+ge}{*(regular)*} The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.

\PY{g+ge}{*(italic)*} \PY{g+ge}{*The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.*}

\PY{g+ge}{*(bold)*} \PY{g+gs}{**The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.**}

\PY{g+ge}{*(highlight)*} \PYZob{}mark\PYZcb{}[The quick brown fox] and \PY{g+ge}{*(keys)*} \PYZob{}keys\PYZcb{}[ctrl+s].

\tscodeinline{\_\_x\_\_} is small caps, not a second bold: that is deviation X1 of the
\href{index.md\#degradation-classes}{degradation classes}, disabled by the \tscodeinline{strict}
profile. \tscodeinline{\_\_} duplicates \tscodeinline{**}, and academic writing needs small caps far more
than a second bold --- and the difference is visible, not silent.
\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

\end{figure}
Delimiters stack for bold italic, and \tscodeinline{\_} never opens emphasis inside a word,
so \tscodeinline{snake\_case\_name} stays literal.
\section{Underline and \tscodeinline{\^{}\^{}x\^{}\^{}}}
Underline has a role and no default sugar: print typography treats the
underline as a typewriter relic, and the obvious spelling \tscodeinline{\_\_x\_\_} went to small
caps instead.

\tscodeinline{\^{}\^{}x\^{}\^{}} --- PyMdownX's caret \enquote{insert} --- is not a TMark construct. With the
feature \tscodeinline{inline.insert} off, which is the default, it is literal text and
raises the hint \tscodeinline{feature-\allowbreak{}off}. Switched on, it is sugar for \tscodeinline{\{underline\}[x]}:
one node whatever the spelling, and the printer emits the role.
\begin{tscode}[lang=md, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}
press:
  features:
\PY{g+gu}{    inline.insert: true}
\PY{g+gu}{\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}}

Now \PYZob{}underline\PYZcb{}[inserted] text, or \PYZca{}\PYZca{}inserted\PYZca{}\PYZca{} in sugar.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
\section{Substitutions}
Three things are rewritten while a run of text is scanned, all of them
producing ordinary text rather than a node:
\begin{itemize}
\item \tscodeinline{(c)}, \tscodeinline{-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}>}, \tscodeinline{1/2} and their siblings, and a \tscodeinline{"straight-\allowbreak{}quoted"} phrase,
which is why quotes reach the PDF as \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}enquote\{…\}} in the document's
language --- see Smart symbols and quotes;
\item \tscodeinline{:smile:}, which expands to the character, while a Material icon shortcode
becomes a web-only span that print drops --- see
Emoji and icons.
\end{itemize}
\tscodeinline{LaTeX}, \tscodeinline{XeLaTeX}, \tscodeinline{BibTeX} and their siblings, written as plain words, are
set as logos by the writers: there is no node and no role, the words stay
ordinary text, and the rule is the feature \tscodeinline{typography.tex-\allowbreak{}logos} (on, whole
words only, never inside code, math, raw passthroughs, link destinations or
attribute values).
\section{Lead-in paragraphs (\tscodeinline{\{lead\}[…]})}
A \emph{lead-in} --- a short run-in heading that opens a paragraph --- has an explicit
role:
\begin{tscode}[lang=md, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PYZob{}lead\PYZcb{}[Boot sequence.] The device powers the flash before the SoC…
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
A paragraph whose \textbf{whole} content is a strong span shorter than 80 characters
is promoted to a lead-in automatically while the \tscodeinline{paragraph.lead} feature is on
(the default; off under \tscodeinline{strict}). A strong span that merely \emph{opens} a
paragraph is a bold run-in and stays one --- promoting it would move the rest of
the sentence into a paragraph of its own, since \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tslead} breaks the paragraph
around the lead-in --- and a bold-only list item is a label, not a lead-in. The
promotion is sugar, not magic: it is named, switchable, and \tscodeinline{tmark lint -\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}fix}
rewrites it to the role. Either way TeXSmith emits \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tslead\{…\}}, defined as:
\begin{tscode}[lang=latex, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}providecommand}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}tslead}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}[1]\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}par}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}noindent}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}textbf}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PYZsh{}1\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}par}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}nobreak}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}smallskip}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
This guarantees a no-indent paragraph break and a small vertical breather, so the label looks identical regardless of what precedes it. Without this, \tscodeinline{**Méthodologie**} wedged between two tables (\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}end\{center\}} ... \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}textbf\{…\}} ... \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}begin\{center\}}) would render flush left while the same construct after running prose would be indented by \tscodeinline{babel-\allowbreak{}french}'s \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}parindent}. With \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tslead}, both cases align.
\begin{tscode}[lang=md, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
La synthèse récapitule, pilier par pilier, les forces et faiblesses…

\PYZob{}lead\PYZcb{}[Sens critique]

| Python | C |
|\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}|\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}|
| Faible | Fort |

\PYZob{}lead\PYZcb{}[Méthodologie]

| Python | C |
|\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}|\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}|
| Correcte | Forte |
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Written as \tscodeinline{**Sens critique**} on a line of its own, the same two labels are
promoted to lead-ins automatically and \tscodeinline{tmark lint} says so
(\tscodeinline{lead-\allowbreak{}promotion}). The rule fires only when the paragraph \emph{is} that strong
span and nothing else, and its plain text is shorter than 80 characters. Bold
spans inside running prose (\tscodeinline{Some **bold** text.}), a run-in such as
\tscodeinline{**Leading bold:** followed by text}, and bold paragraphs over the threshold
all keep their \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}textbf\{…\}} rendering. The \tscodeinline{\{lead\}[…]} role, by contrast, always
takes what follows it on the paragraph, whatever the feature says.

Override \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tslead} in a custom preamble snippet to change the visual style --- for instance, to add a coloured rule, switch to small caps, or replace the \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}smallskip} with \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}medskip}:
\begin{tscode}[lang=latex, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}renewcommand}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}tslead}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}[1]\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}par}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}noindent}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}textsc}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PYZsh{}1\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}par}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}nobreak}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}medskip}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
