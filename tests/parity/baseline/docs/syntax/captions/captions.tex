Markdown doesn't ship with a native caption primitive for figures or tables. The closest thing is image \texttt{alt} text:

\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
![\PY{n+nt}{This is the alt text}](\PY{n+na}{image.png})
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
Alt text exists for accessibility, not for captions. Some browsers show it as a tooltip, but it is not a real caption and you can't style it separately. Moreover, since it is inserted in an HTML tag's attribute, it can't contain block elements or complex formatting.

Fortunately, \texttt{pymdownx.blocks.captions} adds proper caption blocks:

\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in [\PY{n+nt}{this figure}](\PY{n+na}{\PYZsh{}my\PYZhy{}figure}), the results are significant.

![\PY{n+nt}{This is the alt text}](\PY{n+na}{https://picsum.photos/400/150})

/// caption
    attrs: \PYZob{}id: my\PYZhy{}figure\PYZcb{}
This is the caption for the figure.
///
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
\begin{callout}[callout warning]{Identifier restrictions}
The \texttt{id} is declared through the block's YAML options and may not contain
a colon: \texttt{pymdown-\allowbreak{}extensions} rejects identifiers such as
\texttt{fig:my-\allowbreak{}figure}, and the whole block then silently degrades to plain
text (TeXSmith emits a warning when it detects this). The shorthand
header form \texttt{/// caption \#my-\allowbreak{}figure} is likewise not supported by
\texttt{pymdown-\allowbreak{}extensions} --- always use the \texttt{attrs:} option shown above.
\end{callout}
Enable numbering and each document gets its own sequence starting at 1.

\section{LaTeX}\label{latex}

\LaTeX{} wraps figures/tables in \texttt{figure}/\texttt{table} environments, uses \texttt{\textbackslash{}caption\{\}} for the text, and \texttt{\textbackslash{}label\{\}} for cross-references:

\begin{code}{latex}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in Figure \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}ref}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}fig:my\PYZhy{}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}, the results are significant.

\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}begin}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}[htbp]
  \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}centering}
  \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}includegraphics}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}image.png\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
  \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}caption}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}This is the caption for the figure.\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
  \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}label}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}fig:my\PYZhy{}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}end}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
\section{Addressing issues}\label{addressing-issues}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Consistent numbering across a document
\item Easy cross-references
\item Short captions for lists of figures/tables

\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Coherent numbering}\label{coherent-numbering}

Markdown headings aren't numbered, so figures/tables can't piggyback on heading numbering. On the web that's fine---hyperlinks rule the navigation story---but in print numbering is essential. Guideline:

\begin{displayquote}

Printed documents shall have numbered heading elements, figures, and tables for cross-referencing. Web documents, however, should not have numbered headings, figures, or tables, relying instead on hyperlinks for navigation.

\end{displayquote}
Printed \LaTeX{} floats figures and tables, so writers can't assume a caption stays ``above'' or ``below'' the reference. HTML is literal: the figure stays where you put it.

\begin{displayquote}

On printed documents, the words \enquote{above} and \enquote{below} when referring to figures and tables shall never be used, as their position may vary due to floating. On web documents, \enquote{above} and \enquote{below} may be used, as figures and tables appear exactly where they are defined.

\end{displayquote}
\subsection{Cross-referencing captions}\label{cross-referencing-captions}

That rule complicates cross-references: web versions prefer ``this figure below,'' whereas \LaTeX{} wants ``Figure 2.'' Examples:

\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in [\PY{n+nt}{this figure below}](\PY{n+na}{\PYZsh{}my\PYZhy{}figure}), the results are significant.

As seen [\PY{n+nt}{here}](\PY{n+na}{\PYZsh{}my\PYZhy{}figure}), the results are significant.

The results [\PY{n+nt}{shown}](\PY{n+na}{\PYZsh{}my\PYZhy{}figure}) are significant.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
In \LaTeX{} you'd use:

\begin{code}{latex}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in Figure \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}ref}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}fig:my\PYZhy{}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}, the results are significant.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
Language adds another wrinkle: ``Figure'' in English, ``figure'' (lowercase) in French mid-sentence, ``Abbildung'' in German, and so on. Hardcoding wording would be brittle.

Fortunately \texttt{pymdownx.blocks.captions} tracks IDs, so TeXSmith can bridge both worlds with a shared syntax:

\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in [](\PYZsh{}my\PYZhy{}figure), the results are significant.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
An empty-text link to a caption id is decorated with the assigned number. The HTML output looks like:

\begin{code}{html}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in \PY{p}{\PYZlt{}}\PY{n+nt}{a} \PY{n+na}{href}\PY{o}{=}\PY{l+s}{\PYZdq{}\PYZsh{}my\PYZhy{}figure\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{\PYZgt{}}Figure \PY{p}{\PYZlt{}}\PY{n+nt}{span} \PY{n+na}{class}\PY{o}{=}\PY{l+s}{\PYZdq{}caption\PYZhy{}number\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{\PYZgt{}}1\PY{p}{\PYZlt{}}\PY{p}{/}\PY{n+nt}{span}\PY{p}{\PYZgt{}}\PY{p}{\PYZlt{}}\PY{p}{/}\PY{n+nt}{a}\PY{p}{\PYZgt{}}, the results are significant.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
In \LaTeX{} (the caption id is emitted verbatim as the \texttt{\textbackslash{}label}):

\begin{code}{latex}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in Figure \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}ref}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}my\PYZhy{}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}, the results are significant.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
Or with \texttt{cleveref}:

\begin{code}{latex}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As seen in \PY{k}{\PYZbs{}Cref}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}my\PYZhy{}figure\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}, the results are significant.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
Pandoc users write \texttt{\{@fig:my-\allowbreak{}figure\}}; the idea is the same.

\subsection{Short caption names}\label{short-caption-names}

Printed lists of figures appreciate a condensed caption. \LaTeX{} handles this via the optional \texttt{\textbackslash{}caption[]} argument:

\begin{code}{latex}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}caption}\PY{n+na}{[Short caption for list of figures]}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}This is the caption for the figure.\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
TeXSmith reuses the Markdown \texttt{alt} text as that short entry:

\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
![\PY{n+nt}{Short caption for list of figures}](\PY{n+na}{image.png})

/// figure\PYZhy{}caption
    attrs: \PYZob{}id: my\PYZhy{}figure\PYZcb{}
This is the caption for the figure.
///
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
\section{Caption lines}\label{caption-lines}

The block form is verbose, and its id may not contain a colon. The caption
\emph{line} is the shorthand: the paragraph right after the float, spelled
\texttt{Kind: text \{\#id\}}, with \texttt{Figure:}, \texttt{Table:} or \texttt{Listing:} as the kind. This
is TMark's canonical spelling, and the one \texttt{tmark fmt} prints.

\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
![\PY{n+nt}{Short caption for the list of figures}](\PY{n+na}{image.png})\PYZob{}width=70\PYZpc{}\PYZcb{}

Figure: This is the caption for the figure. \PYZob{}\PYZsh{}fig:my\PYZhy{}figure\PYZcb{}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
| Header 1 | Header 2 |
|\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}|\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}|
| Cell 1   | Cell 2   |

Table: This is the caption for the table. \PYZob{}\PYZsh{}tbl:my\PYZhy{}table\PYZcb{}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
\begin{code}{md}{}{}
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{l+s+sb}{```}\PY{l+s+sb}{python}
\PY{k}{def}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nf}{bubble\PYZus{}sort}\PY{p}{(}\PY{n}{items}\PY{p}{)}\PY{p}{:} \PY{o}{.}\PY{o}{.}\PY{o}{.}
\PY{l+s+sb}{```}

Listing: Bubble sort, naive version. \PYZob{}\PYZsh{}lst:bubble\PYZcb{}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{code}
The attribute list is optional and may be a full one (\texttt{\{\#fig:plot .wide\}});
its \texttt{\#id} is the float's anchor, and ids with a prefix (\texttt{fig:}, \texttt{tbl:},
\texttt{lst:}) are fine here. The image \texttt{alt} stays the short caption. A \texttt{Figure:}
line renders exactly like the \texttt{/// caption} block above; a \texttt{Listing:} line
makes the code block a numbered listing whose caption is the block's title
and whose id is its label, in \LaTeX{} as in Typst.

The line attaches to the float \emph{before} it when that float is of the matching
kind and has no caption yet, otherwise to the float \emph{after} it --- so the
\texttt{Table:} line before its table keeps working. A \texttt{Figure:} line under a table,
or a caption line with no float next to it, is left as a plain paragraph.
