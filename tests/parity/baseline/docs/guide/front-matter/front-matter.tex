Every Markdown document can carry a small block of metadata at the very top, fenced by \tscodeinline{-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}} markers. This is the \textbf{front matter}: a YAML island living rent-free above your prose. Static site generators like MkDocs read it to drive per-page configuration, and TeXSmith hooks into the same convention to steer how a document is parsed, typeset, and ultimately rendered to \LaTeX{} or PDF.

A guiding principle: \textbf{keep content and form apart}. Templates, font sizes, margins, paper format, and other typographic knobs belong in the front matter; the body should care only about ideas, sentences, and equations. Other tools take a different stance, see \href{https://quarkdown.com/}{Quarkdown}, which weaves configuration directly into the document body. Both are valid, but TeXSmith favors the separation, your future self will thank you when swapping templates without touching a single paragraph.
\section{The shape of the front matter}
Document metadata stays at the \textbf{root}, because that is where MkDocs, Pandoc
and editors read it. Everything TeXSmith owns may sit under \tscodeinline{press:} --- a
namespace, not a category, whose only job is to keep those keys out of a static
site generator's way. When the same key appears in both places, \tscodeinline{press} wins.

Four groups, and the split is the same one the body follows: \tscodeinline{declare} says a
\enquote{Solution} callout \emph{exists}, belongs to the group \enquote{Solutions} and is referred to
as \enquote{See page N}; the form keys (\tscodeinline{template}, \tscodeinline{callouts}, \tscodeinline{code}, ...) say it is
blue with a graduation-cap icon. A document is re-skinned by replacing the form
keys alone.

All sections are validated with pydantic: an unknown key fails at parse time,
not in the PDF.
\begin{tscallout}[kind=note, title={The 0.6 layout is deprecated}]
Top-level \tscodeinline{bibliography:}, \tscodeinline{crossrefs:}, \tscodeinline{counters:}, \tscodeinline{glossary:},
\tscodeinline{acronyms:} and \tscodeinline{admonitions:} are still read, with a deprecation warning
naming the replacement. \tscodeinline{tmark lint -\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}fix FILE} moves them; the table is in
Migrating to TMark.
\end{tscallout}
\section{Moustaches}
Any front-matter value is available in the body as \tscodeinline{\{\{ key \}\}} or
\tscodeinline{\{\{ press.template \}\}}, resolved after parsing and never inside code spans or
fenced blocks. Moustaches are substitution, not templating: no logic, no loops,
no filters. An unresolved moustache warns and is left in place, visibly.
\section{Press}
The \tscodeinline{press} block holds everything related to the printed (or PDF'd) artifact: the template choice, typographic options, and template-specific slots.

\section{Bibliography}
References can be declared inline, right next to the document that cites them, no external \tscodeinline{.bib} file required (though one still works if you prefer). Mix DOI shortcuts with fully-spelled-out entries as needed:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{sources}\PY{p}{:}

\PY{+w}{      }\PY{n+nt}{AB2020}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{doi:10.1000/xyz123}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{n+nt}{CD2019}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{type}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{book}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{author}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+s}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{l+s}{John}\PY{n+nv}{ }\PY{l+s}{Doe}\PY{l+s}{\PYZdq{}}

\section{Counters}
Document-specific numbered series --- findings, requirements, bugs, test cases ---
are declared under \tscodeinline{press.declare.counters}. Each key is the prefix used by the
\tscodeinline{\#(…)} markers and the \tscodeinline{@…} references in the body:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]

\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{declare}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{counters}\PY{p}{:}

\section{Cross-document references}
\tscodeinline{id} gives the document a free identifier (a contract or report number);
\tscodeinline{press.sources.crossrefs} declares the inventories published by the documents it
cites:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nt}{id}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{RHE\PYZhy{}424}
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{sources}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{crossrefs}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{n+nt}{fwrev}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{build/firmware\PYZhy{}review.refs.json}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
A citation then reads \tscodeinline{@fwrev:fw:pas-\allowbreak{}de-\allowbreak{}temps} and renders as
\tscodeinline{RHE-\allowbreak{}423-\allowbreak{}FW-\allowbreak{}10 p. 14}. See Cross-document references.
\section{Features}
Every switchable behaviour has a dotted name and a default. \tscodeinline{press.features}
flips entries, and nothing else in the front matter does.
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{features}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{figures.exec}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{true}\PY{+w}{        }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} execute `python image` fences}
