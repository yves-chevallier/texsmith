TeXSmith reads bibliographic data from \tslogo{BibTeX} files and from YAML front matter. Use it to keep citations and references tidy in academic writing, technical docs, or any project that wants repeatable citation management.
\section{Using Bibliography Files}
Pass one or more \tscodeinline{.bib} files on the command line:
\begin{tscode}[lang=bash, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
texsmith\PY{+w}{ }docs/chapter.md\PY{+w}{ }references.bib
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
You can also add \tscodeinline{file1.bib file2.bib} as positional inputs alongside a MkDocs site so every page sees the same pool of references.
\section{Using the front matter}
You can declare bibliography entries directly in the YAML front matter of your Markdown documents:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{sources}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{bibliography}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} Extract citation from DOI}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{n+nt}{citation\PYZhy{}keyword}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{https://doi.org/10.1000/xyz123}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} Manual bibliography entry}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{n+nt}{AI2027}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{type}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{misc}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{title}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{AI}\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{2027}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{date}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{2025\PYZhy{}04\PYZhy{}03}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{url}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{https://ai\PYZhy{}2027.com/ai\PYZhy{}2027.pdf}
\PY{+w}{        }\PY{n+nt}{authors}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{          }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Daniel}\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Kokotajlo}
\PY{+w}{          }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Scott}\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Alexander}
\PY{+w}{          }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Thomas}\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Larsen}
\PY{+w}{          }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nt}{first}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Eli}
\PY{+w}{            }\PY{n+nt}{last}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Lifland}
\PY{+w}{          }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Romeo}\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{Dean}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
A top-level \tscodeinline{bibliography:} key is the deprecated spelling; \tscodeinline{tmark lint -\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}fix}
moves it under \tscodeinline{press.sources}.

The format mirrors \tslogo{BibTeX}, translated to YAML by \tscodeinline{pybtex}.

Two approaches:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Provide a DOI link; TeXSmith resolves it into a full \tslogo{BibTeX} entry.
\item Provide a manual entry with the fields you need.
\end{enumerate}
See the \hyperref[cheese]{academic paper} example or the \hyperref[einstein]{book} example.
\section{Citation Syntax}
A citation is the \tscodeinline{@} sigil against the bibliography registry --- the same sigil
that refers to a figure or a section, because the registry decides what the key
means.
\begin{tscode}[lang=md, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}
press:
  sources:
    bibliography:
\PY{g+gu}{      WADHWANI20111713: https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2010\PYZhy{}3952}
\PY{g+gu}{\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}}

\PY{g+gh}{\PYZsh{} Introduction}

Cheese exhibits unique melting properties \PY{n+ni}{@WADHWANI20111713}.
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Which renders into:
\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{snippet-<HASH>.pdf}
\caption[Demo]{Demo}
\end{figure}
A bare \tscodeinline{@key} is the short, parenthetical citation by default and
\tscodeinline{@[key, locator]} the same short form with a locator attached:
\begin{tscode}[lang=md, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
As shown by @[WADHWANI20111713, p. 33], and elsewhere
@[see WADHWANI20111713, pp. 33\PYZhy{}35; AI2027, ch. 1]. Suppress the author with
@[\PYZhy{}WADHWANI20111713], and cite several at once with @[AI2027; WADHWANI20111713].
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Locators follow Pandoc: a recognised locator word (\tscodeinline{p.}, \tscodeinline{pp.}, \tscodeinline{ch.}, \tscodeinline{sec.},
\tscodeinline{§}...) followed by a range, or free suffix text. Pandoc's own \tscodeinline{[@key, locator]}
is accepted for import and never emitted.

Setting \tscodeinline{press.features: \{citations.narrative: true\}} in the front matter
switches every bare \tscodeinline{@key} of the document to the narrative form instead
(\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}textcite} in \LaTeX{}, \tscodeinline{\#cite(<key>, form: "prose")} in Typst --- \enquote{Einstein [3]}
rather than \enquote{[3]}). Either form stays reachable per citation regardless of the
document's default: \tscodeinline{@[+key]} forces the narrative form inside brackets,
\tscodeinline{@[-\allowbreak{}key]} forces the year alone.

A DOI can be cited in place through the predeclared \tscodeinline{doi} prefix, without a
front-matter entry: \tscodeinline{@doi:10.3168/jds.2010-\allowbreak{}3952}. The same DOI cited twice is
one entry. \tscodeinline{@https://doi.org/…} is accepted as sugar and normalised.

Resolution order for any \tscodeinline{@key} is: declared counter prefix (\tscodeinline{doi} and \tscodeinline{gls}
included), then the bibliography. A key present in two registries is a hard
warning, and an unresolved key renders visibly as \tscodeinline{[?key]}.
\begin{tscallout}[kind=note, title={The footnote spelling is deprecated}]
TeXSmith 0.6 spelled citations \tscodeinline{[\^{}key]} and \tscodeinline{\^{}[k1,k2]}. Both are still
parsed with a deprecation warning, and while they last the
footnote-versus-citation shadowing rule is preserved: a real footnote with
the same key wins. Once they are retired, \tscodeinline{\^{}[…]} becomes an inline
footnote. \tscodeinline{tmark lint -\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}fix} rewrites them; see
Migrating to TMark.
\end{tscallout}
\section{\tslogo{BibTeX}}
\tslogo{BibTeX} is an old, loosely specified format with many dialects (\tscodeinline{bibtex}, \tscodeinline{bibtex8}, \tscodeinline{pbibtex}, and more). The most complete parser is \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biber\_(LaTeX)}{biber}, but it is Perl-based and not embeddable. TeXSmith relies on \href{https://pybtex.org/}{pybtex}, which covers the common cases.
