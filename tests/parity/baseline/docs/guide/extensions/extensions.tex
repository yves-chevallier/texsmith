\title{Extending TeXSmith}
\author{}\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
TeXSmith's dialect is TMark, and TMark is a \textbf{closed}
language: the four syntactic families, the role registry and the container
registry do not grow per project. That is the point --- a construct you have never
seen is still readable, and a document still renders somewhere else.

So \enquote{writing an extension} is not \enquote{adding syntax}. It is one of four things:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
\textbf{You want to...} & \textbf{Do this} \\
\midrule
add a new \emph{kind} of callout (Solution, Exercise, Risk) & declare it under \tscodeinline{press.declare.admonitions} \\
add a new numbered series (findings, requirements) & declare it under \tscodeinline{press.declare.counters} \\
wrap content in something the template styles & use \tscodeinline{::: div \{.class\}} and style the class \\
compute something at build time (fetch, convert, execute) & write an \textbf{IR pass} \\
change how a construct \emph{looks} & redefine its \textbf{contract macro}, or replace the fragment \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The first two are pure front matter and need no code:
\href{../syntax/admonitions.md\#custom-types}{Admonitions} and
Custom counters.
\section{Containers}
A container is the escape hatch for structure. The container names TMark knows
are a \textbf{closed} registry --- the callout types (built-in and declared), \tscodeinline{aside},
\tscodeinline{figure}, \tscodeinline{tabs}, \tscodeinline{tab}, \tscodeinline{multicolumn} and \tscodeinline{div} --- and there is deliberately no
mechanism to add a name: a template that renders a name the registry does not
know has extended the language.

\tscodeinline{::: div} is the container that means nothing: a hook for classes and an id.
\begin{tscode}[lang=md, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
::: div \PYZob{}\PYZsh{}s1 .sidebar\PYZcb{}
Content the template lays out.
:::
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Every layout container lowers to one contract on the paged backends ---
\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}begin\{tsdiv\}\{name\}[attrs]}, \tscodeinline{\#ts-\allowbreak{}div("name", ..)} --- dispatched on the name,
with \tscodeinline{\#id} forwarded as \tscodeinline{id}, classes as \tscodeinline{class=\{a,b\}} and \tscodeinline{key=val} as is
(\tscodeinline{lang} and \tscodeinline{media} never). Style the class:
\begin{tscode}[lang=latex, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}tcbset}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}/ts/div/class/sidebar/.style=\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}grow to left by=2cm\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
On the web the same container is \tscodeinline{<div class="sidebar">}, which is exactly what
a site stylesheet needs. A name the registry does not know raises
\tscodeinline{container-\allowbreak{}unknown} and renders its content transparently, rather than silently
becoming an unstyled \tscodeinline{<div>}.
\section{IR passes}
Anything that has to \emph{compute} --- read a file, convert a diagram, call a
network service, execute a fence --- is a pass: a pure function
\tscodeinline{(Document, PassContext) -\allowbreak{}> Document} over the IR, running either before
\tscodeinline{tmark.resolve} (\tscodeinline{pre}) or after it (\tscodeinline{post}).
\begin{tscode}[lang=python, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k+kn}{from}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nn}{texsmith}\PY{n+nn}{.}\PY{n+nn}{core}\PY{n+nn}{.}\PY{n+nn}{documents}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{k+kn}{import} \PY{n}{Document}
\PY{k+kn}{from}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nn}{texsmith}\PY{n+nn}{.}\PY{n+nn}{passes}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{k+kn}{import} \PY{n}{PassContext}\PY{p}{,} \PY{n}{spec}

\PY{n+nd}{@spec}\PY{p}{(}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{l+s+s2}{exam\PYZhy{}questions}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{,} \PY{n}{stage}\PY{o}{=}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{l+s+s2}{pre}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{,} \PY{n}{after}\PY{o}{=}\PY{p}{(}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{l+s+s2}{include}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{,}\PY{p}{)}\PY{p}{)}
\PY{k}{def}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nf}{run}\PY{p}{(}\PY{n}{document}\PY{p}{:} \PY{n}{Document}\PY{p}{,} \PY{n}{ctx}\PY{p}{:} \PY{n}{PassContext}\PY{p}{)} \PY{o}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{o}{\PYZgt{}} \PY{n}{Document}\PY{p}{:}
    \PY{o}{.}\PY{o}{.}\PY{o}{.}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Declare a template's passes in its manifest so they apply only while that
template renders (\tscodeinline{[typst.template]} reads the same key):
\begin{tscode}[lang=toml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k}{[}\PY{k}{latex}\PY{k}{.}\PY{k}{template}\PY{k}{]}
\PY{n}{passes}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{o}{=}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{p}{[}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{l+s+s2}{my\PYZus{}exam\PYZus{}pkg.questions:run}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{]}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
The entry names a \tscodeinline{PassSpec}, a callable registered by \tscodeinline{@spec(...)} --- its
registered spec is reused --- or a plain callable, and a typo fails when the
template loads, with a \tscodeinline{TemplateError} naming the template and the entry. The
pass reads the template's attributes from its context:
\tscodeinline{ctx.attribute("solution", False)} is \tscodeinline{True} under \tscodeinline{-\allowbreak{}a solution=true}.

The full contract --- ordering, diagnostics, the rules a pass must not break --- is
in IR passes and fragment contracts.
\section{Fragments and contract macros}
The writers emit a fixed macro or environment per construct (\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tslead},
\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsaside}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tskeys}, \tscodeinline{tscallout}, \tscodeinline{tscode}, ...), and a fragment must define
every macro the writer can name. Restyle through the \tscodeinline{pgfkeys} family, redefine
the macro with the same signature, or replace the fragment outright:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{fragments}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{disable}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{p+pIndicator}{[}\PY{n+nv}{ts\PYZhy{}code}\PY{p+pIndicator}{]}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{append}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{p+pIndicator}{[}\PY{n+nv}{./my\PYZhy{}code.sty}\PY{p+pIndicator}{]}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
See Contract macros for the construct-to-macro table
and Fragments for how a fragment is built.

On a MkDocs site the index entries reach \tscodeinline{search\_index.json} through the
\tscodeinline{texsmith} plugin, which collects them from the rendered pages
and injects them after the \tscodeinline{search} plugin wrote its index. The separate
\tscodeinline{texsmith.index} MkDocs \emph{plugin} is deprecated --- it warns and does nothing, and
disappears in 0.8; and the \tscodeinline{texsmith.index} \emph{Markdown extension} went with the
Python-Markdown pipeline.
\begin{tscallout}[kind=note, title={The Python-Markdown extensions are gone}]
TeXSmith shipped 17 Python-Markdown extensions (\tscodeinline{smallcaps}, \tscodeinline{latex\_raw},
\tscodeinline{multi\_citations}, \tscodeinline{mermaid}, \tscodeinline{index}, \tscodeinline{counters}, ...) and registered them as
\tscodeinline{markdown.extensions} entry points. They are removed in 0.8.0 together with
the spellings they implemented: tmark parses every Markdown source, and a
site that listed \tscodeinline{texsmith.*} under \tscodeinline{markdown\_extensions} must drop those
entries. See Migrating to TMark.
\end{tscallout}
\section{Inspecting what the parser read}
\begin{tscode}[lang=bash, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
tmark\PY{+w}{ }parse\PY{+w}{ }report.md\PY{+w}{      }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} the IR as JSON}
tmark\PY{+w}{ }check\PY{+w}{ }\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}strict\PY{+w}{ }FILE\PY{+w}{  }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} parse, resolve and lint}
tmark\PY{+w}{ }write\PY{+w}{ }\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}to\PY{+w}{ }latex\PY{+w}{ }\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}map\PY{+w}{ }FILE\PY{+w}{  }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} the body, its source map and its Requires}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
\tscodeinline{Requires} is what a construct demands of the template: packages, fragments,
shell escape, assets, citations, index registries, counter series. If your
extension needs the template to do something, that is where it says so.
