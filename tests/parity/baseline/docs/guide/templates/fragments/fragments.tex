\title{Template fragments}
\author{}\date{}

\maketitle
Fragments are small, pluggable \LaTeX{} packages (\tscodeinline{.sty} rendered from Jinja) that
TeXSmith can inject into any template at \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}VAR\{extra\_packages\}}. They keep
shared logic (callouts, code listings, ...) out of individual templates
while staying configurable from front matter or your own extensions.
\section{Built-in fragments}
Most built-in fragments are \textbf{contract fragments}: they define the macros
the tmark writer emits for a construct (\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsmark}, \tscodeinline{tscallout}, \tscodeinline{tscode},
\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tskeys}, ...), listed per fragment in \tscodeinline{tmark.fragments()} and documented in
Contract macros. A contract fragment renders when the writer
names it in \tscodeinline{Requires.fragments} (on the legacy Jinja path, when its macros
appear in the rendered content).
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}geometry}}] page size/orientation glue that mirrors \tscodeinline{press.paper}/\tscodeinline{press.geometry} options.
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}typesetting}}] paragraph spacing, leading and line numbers (inline, when configured), and the
\end{description}
contract macros \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tslead}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsmark}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsdivider}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsepigraph}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsaside},
\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsprogress}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsicon} and the \tscodeinline{tsdiv} container (\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}typesetting.sty}, when
the writer requires it).
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}fonts}}] font selection driven by \tscodeinline{fonts.family}, script fallback fonts, and the
\end{description}
\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsscript} / \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsemoji} switches.
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}extra}}] aux packages: \tscodeinline{Requires.packages} of the writer merged with the packages the
\end{description}
active contract fragments imply (on the legacy path, detected from the
rendered content: hyperref, soul, ulem, etc.).
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}keystrokes}}] \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tskeys\{Ctrl,Alt,Del\}} (and the legacy \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}keystroke\{…\}}) as styled TikZ boxes.
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}callouts}}] the \tscodeinline{tscallout} environment (and the legacy \tscodeinline{callout} box) generated from the
\end{description}
callout definitions and \tscodeinline{press.callouts.*}.
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}code}}] the \tscodeinline{tscode} environment and \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tscodeinline}, over minted, listings or
\end{description}
fvextra according to \tscodeinline{code.engine}.
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}critic}}] critic markup: \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsins}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsdel}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tssubst}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tscomment}.
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}index}}] \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsindex}, central imakeidx glue, one \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}makeindex[name=…]} per registry of
\end{description}
\tscodeinline{Requires.index}, texindy/makeindex selection.
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}glossary}}] \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsgls} / \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsacr} and the glossary wiring: loads \tscodeinline{glossaries}, runs \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}makeglossaries} when needed, and materializes acronym definitions from front matter with configurable styles.
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}bibliography}}] bibliography helper that wires \tscodeinline{biblatex} into the rendered document.
\end{description}
By default, raw URLs in entries are suppressed and the entry title becomes
a clickable hyperlink to the entry's \tscodeinline{url} field --- this avoids the
overfull/underfull \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}hbox} warnings that long URLs typically cause in
justified bibliographies. Set \tscodeinline{bibliography\_show\_urls: true} in the
document front matter to print the full URL inline instead (same behaviour
as biblatex's stock styles).
\begin{description}
\item[{\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}todolist}}] checklist helpers providing \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}done}, \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}wontfix}, and the \tscodeinline{todolist} environment when they are referenced.
\end{description}
All built-in templates default to rendering these fragments. They are
written into the build directory as \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}*.sty} and loaded via
\tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}usepackage\{...\}} in the generated \TeX{}.
\section{Using fragments in documents}
Fragments are declared under the \tscodeinline{press.fragments} key in front matter. Each
entry is either the name of a built-in fragment or a path to a custom Jinja
template (absolute or relative to the Markdown file).
\subsection{Explicit list (replaces template defaults)}
Provide a plain list to replace the template's default fragment set entirely:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nn}{\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}}
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{template}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{article}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{fragments}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ts\PYZhy{}callouts}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ts\PYZhy{}code}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ts\PYZhy{}glossary}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{fragments/foo.jinja.sty}\PY{+w}{  }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} custom fragment located next to your doc}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{foo}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{value}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{42}\PY{+w}{         }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} variables consumed by foo.jinja.sty}
\PY{n+nn}{\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
\subsection{Modifier dict (extends template defaults)}
When you only need to add or remove a few fragments without restating the full
list, use the dict form with any combination of \tscodeinline{append}, \tscodeinline{prepend}, and
\tscodeinline{disable}:
\begin{tscode}[lang=yaml, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{n+nn}{\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}}
\PY{n+nt}{press}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{template}\PY{p}{:}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{article}
\PY{+w}{  }\PY{n+nt}{fragments}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{append}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{./fragments/heiglogo}\PY{+w}{   }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} add a custom fragment at the end}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{prepend}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{./fragments/header}\PY{+w}{     }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} add a fragment at the start}
\PY{+w}{    }\PY{n+nt}{disable}\PY{p}{:}
\PY{+w}{      }\PY{p+pIndicator}{\PYZhy{}}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{l+lScalar+lScalarPlain}{ts\PYZhy{}geometry}\PY{+w}{            }\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} remove a specific built\PYZhy{}in fragment}
\PY{n+nn}{\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}\PYZhy{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
All three keys are optional. Omitted keys leave the corresponding part of the
default list unchanged. \tscodeinline{disable} is applied first (before \tscodeinline{prepend}/\tscodeinline{append}),
so the same fragment cannot appear in both \tscodeinline{disable} and \tscodeinline{prepend}/\tscodeinline{append}.

TeXSmith renders each fragment into the output directory and injects the
corresponding \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}usepackage\{…\}} lines into \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}VAR\{extra\_packages\}}.
\subsection{What a fragment looks like}
Fragments are plain Jinja templates that output \LaTeX{}. The package name is the
stem of the file unless it is a built-in registered name.
\begin{tscode}[lang=tex, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{c}{\PYZpc{} fragments/foo.jinja.sty}
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}ProvidesPackage}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}foo\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}[2025/01/01 Example fragment]
\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}newcommand}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}FooValue}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}\PY{k}{\PYZbs{}VAR}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZob{}}foo.value|default(0)\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}\PY{n+nb}{\PYZcb{}}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
With the front matter above, the generated build will contain \tscodeinline{foo.sty} and the
document preamble will include \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}usepackage\{foo\}}.
\section{Template authors: allowing fragments}
To consume fragments, a template needs a placeholder where TeXSmith can inject
the \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}usepackage} lines. Add \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}VAR\{extra\_packages\}} near the top of your
preamble---typically next to other package imports. No TOML manifest changes are
required; the core runtime resolves fragments before rendering.

Built-in templates already include this placeholder and opt into
\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}geometry}, \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}extra}, \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}keystrokes}, \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}callouts}, \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}code},
\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}glossary}, \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}index}, \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}bibliography}, and \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}todolist} via the template
runtime extras; conditional fragments only render when their macros are present
in the rendered \LaTeX{}. Third-party templates can also declare default fragments
in their \tscodeinline{TemplateRuntime.extras["fragments"]} or let users supply their own
through front matter.
\subsection{Passing variables to fragments}
Any values under \tscodeinline{press} (or other front matter keys) are merged into the
template rendering context. If your fragment expects a variable such as
\tscodeinline{foo.value}, document it and read it directly in the Jinja template. Unknown
keys are ignored.
\section{CLI and API integration}
CLI usage is automatic once \tscodeinline{press.fragments} is present. For API consumers:
\begin{tscode}[lang=python, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
\PY{k+kn}{from}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nn}{texsmith}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{k+kn}{import} \PY{n}{Document}\PY{p}{,} \PY{n}{TemplateSession}
\PY{k+kn}{from}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{n+nn}{texsmith}\PY{n+nn}{.}\PY{n+nn}{core}\PY{n+nn}{.}\PY{n+nn}{templates}\PY{+w}{ }\PY{k+kn}{import} \PY{n}{load\PYZus{}template\PYZus{}runtime}

\PY{n}{session} \PY{o}{=} \PY{n}{TemplateSession}\PY{p}{(}\PY{n}{load\PYZus{}template\PYZus{}runtime}\PY{p}{(}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{l+s+s2}{article}\PY{l+s+s2}{\PYZdq{}}\PY{p}{)}\PY{p}{)}
\PY{n}{session}\PY{o}{.}\PY{n}{add\PYZus{}document}\PY{p}{(}\PY{n}{Document}\PY{o}{.}\PY{n}{from\PYZus{}markdown}\PY{p}{(}\PY{n}{path\PYZus{}to\PYZus{}md}\PY{p}{)}\PY{p}{)}
\PY{n}{result} \PY{o}{=} \PY{n}{session}\PY{o}{.}\PY{n}{render}\PY{p}{(}\PY{n}{output\PYZus{}dir}\PY{p}{)}
\PY{c+c1}{\PYZsh{} result.main\PYZus{}tex\PYZus{}path already includes the rendered fragments}
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Custom fragments may live anywhere; relative paths are resolved against the
document's directory. Built-in names are always available without shipping
assets in your template package.
