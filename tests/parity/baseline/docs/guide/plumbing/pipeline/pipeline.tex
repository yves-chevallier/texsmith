\title{How does TeXSmith work?}
\author{}\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
TeXSmith ingests \textbf{Markdown} (\tscodeinline{.md}), \textbf{YAML} (\tscodeinline{.yaml}) and \textbf{\tslogo{BibTeX}}
(\tscodeinline{.bib}), then runs them through a conversion pipeline to produce \LaTeX{}, Typst,
or a finished PDF.

Templates define the layout and expose slots that get filled with content from
your sources. The template also relies on \textbf{fragments} --- extra layers that add
a bibliography, glossary, fonts, page geometry, or other typesetting options.

\section{The shape of the pipeline}
\begin{tscode}[lang=text, stretch=0.5, engine=pygments]
\begin{Verbatim}[commandchars=\\\{\},breaklines, breakanywhere, commandchars=\\\{\}]
.md ──tmark.parse──▶ IR ──TeXSmith passes──▶ IR\PYZsq{} ──tmark.resolve(loader)──▶ Resolved
                     pre:  include · glossary · var · title · snippet ·
                           assets · doi · emoji · scripts
                     post: slots · headings · highlight
                                                                          │
      ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
      ▼
  tmark.write(IR\PYZsq{}, \PYZdq{}latex\PYZdq{} | \PYZdq{}typst\PYZdq{} | \PYZdq{}html\PYZdq{}) ──▶ Body \PYZob{} text, map, requires \PYZcb{}
      │
      └─▶ Requires → fragments → preamble; text → template slots; engines → PDF
\end{Verbatim}
\end{tscode}
Two rules keep the boundary honest. A function that needs a file, a clock, a
process or a socket is \textbf{TeXSmith's} (or hides behind a \tscodeinline{Loader}). A writer
that needs to know the template, the font, the packages or the geometry is
asking for a \textbf{fragment contract}, not an option.

\section{Internal pipeline}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Collect and classify inputs.}
The CLI and \tscodeinline{ConversionService} accept Markdown documents, optional
front matter YAML, and bibliography files. \tscodeinline{split\_inputs} peels off
\tscodeinline{.bib}/\tscodeinline{.bibtex}, treats a lone YAML file as the only document when needed,
and normalises any provided front matter. When documents share front matter,
it is deep-merged into each \tscodeinline{Document}, with \tscodeinline{press.*} metadata validated up
front to avoid surprises later.
\item \textbf{Parse to the IR.}
\tscodeinline{tmark.parse} reads the source and produces the intermediate representation:
a typed tree with a committed JSON schema, plus the front matter and a source
span for every node. \tscodeinline{texsmith.ir.model} is the generated Python mirror of
that schema, so the Python side never hand-writes the node catalogue. Parse
diagnostics --- including the deprecation warnings of
Migrating to TMark --- land in the render's
\tscodeinline{DiagnosticSink}.
\item \textbf{Run the \tscodeinline{pre} passes.}
A pass is a pure function \tscodeinline{(Document, PassContext) -\allowbreak{}> Document} over the
generated models: it never mutates its input, returns the same object when it
has nothing to do, and never raises --- a failure becomes a visible literal in
the output plus a diagnostic at the node's span. Most \tscodeinline{pre} passes need I/O,
which is exactly why they are Python's. In their declared order:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
\textbf{Pass} & \textbf{I/O} & \textbf{What it does} \\
\midrule
\tscodeinline{include} & yes & splices \tscodeinline{\{include\}(file)} and fence \tscodeinline{include=} sources, rebasing relative paths \\
\tscodeinline{glossary} &  & validates the structured \tscodeinline{press.declare.glossary} section and declares one abbreviation per entry, so the prose substitutes the key and the backmatter lists it \\
\tscodeinline{var} &  & expands \tscodeinline{\{\{ key \}\}} moustaches against the front matter \\
\tscodeinline{title} &  & promotes the first heading to the document title \\
\tscodeinline{snippet} & yes & renders \tscodeinline{.snippet} fences into preview figures \\
\tscodeinline{assets} & yes & converts and hashes images and diagrams (mermaid, draw.io, SVG) \\
\tscodeinline{doi} & yes & fetches pending DOIs into a generated \tscodeinline{.bib} \\
\tscodeinline{emoji} & yes & picks the emoji rendering mode and its assets \\
\tscodeinline{scripts} & yes & detects non-Latin runs and chooses fallback font families \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Order is declared, not implicit: each pass registers a \tscodeinline{PassSpec} with
\tscodeinline{after=}, and \tscodeinline{build\_pipeline} performs a stable topological sort that raises
\tscodeinline{PassOrderError} on a cycle --- so the table reads as a listing, not as a
contract, and the constraints are what actually hold (\tscodeinline{assets} after
\tscodeinline{include} and \tscodeinline{snippet}, \tscodeinline{emoji} after \tscodeinline{assets}, \tscodeinline{scripts} after \tscodeinline{var} and
\tscodeinline{emoji}). A template or plugin registers its own the same way, and a \tscodeinline{pre}
pass declared \tscodeinline{after} a \tscodeinline{post} pass is refused. \tscodeinline{DEFAULT\_PIPELINE} in
\tscodeinline{texsmith.passes} is the list the CLI runs.
\item \textbf{Bind the template and attributes.}
\tscodeinline{bind\_template} resolves which template runtime to use and which slots exist.
Template attributes declared in \tscodeinline{manifest.toml} are merged in a strict order:
template defaults \tsscript{symbols}{\(\rightarrow\) }fragment defaults \tsscript{symbols}{\(\rightarrow\) }front matter (\tscodeinline{press.*}) \tsscript{symbols}{\(\rightarrow\)}
CLI/session overrides. Attribute ownership is enforced so two fragments (or
the template) cannot claim the same attribute.
\item \textbf{Resolve once.}
\tscodeinline{tmark.resolve} walks the whole document with a \tscodeinline{Loader} supplied by
TeXSmith and turns every anchor, reference, citation and counter item into a
resolved label: \enquote{Figure 3}, \enquote{FW-01}, a citation key, a glossary entry, a
cross-document inventory hit. Resolution happens \textbf{once}, over the whole
document, so numbering is consistent across slots and unresolved references
are reported with their span instead of silently disappearing.
\item \textbf{Run the \tscodeinline{post} passes.}
These only slice the block list or compute per-body options, so the
\tscodeinline{Resolved} of the whole document stays valid: \tscodeinline{slots} splits the tree into
the template's slots, \tscodeinline{headings} computes the per-slot heading offset, and
\tscodeinline{highlight} renders Pygments payloads for the \LaTeX{} backend.
\item \textbf{Write one body per slot.}
\tscodeinline{tmark.write(ir, backend)} emits the body text for \tscodeinline{latex}, \tscodeinline{typst} or
\tscodeinline{html}, together with a \textbf{source map} (for SyncTeX) and a \tscodeinline{Requires}
record. \tscodeinline{Requires} is the writer's demand on the template, and it is what
makes the boundary work: the packages the body needs, the \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}*} fragments
that must provide its contract macros, whether shell escape is required, the
assets to copy, whether a bibliography exists, and the cited keys, acronyms,
index registries and counter series actually used.
\item \textbf{Activate fragments from \tscodeinline{Requires}.}
The unioned \tscodeinline{Requires} of every slot drives fragment activation: a body that
emits \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tscallout} pulls in \tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}callouts}, one that emits \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}tsindex} pulls in
\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}index}, and so on. A macro the writer names, a fragment must define --- the
check is by construction rather than by sniffing the rendered \LaTeX{}. See
Contract macros.
\item \textbf{Fonts, bibliography and index.}
The script usage collected by the \tscodeinline{scripts} pass chooses per-script font
families and emits font-switching commands; \tscodeinline{-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}fonts-\allowbreak{}info} surfaces the
detected scripts and counts. Only the cited keys are written to the
generated \tscodeinline{texsmith-\allowbreak{}bibliography.bib}, keeping outputs lean. The index
registries listed in \tscodeinline{Requires} decide which \tscodeinline{\textbackslash{}printindex} blocks the
\tscodeinline{ts-\allowbreak{}index} fragment drops into the backmatter.
\item \textbf{Template wrap and emission.}
Slot bodies are merged back into the template entrypoint by
\tscodeinline{wrap\_template\_document}, alongside template/fragment attributes, required
assets, and optional manifest/debug artefacts. The resulting
\tscodeinline{TemplateRenderResult} carries the main \tscodeinline{.tex} (or \tscodeinline{.typ}) path, per-fragment
outputs, the bibliography path, the selected engine and its shell-escape
requirement, ready for Tectonic, latexmk or the Typst compiler to produce the
final PDF. Each conversion also publishes its \tscodeinline{.refs.json}
inventory for other documents to cite.
\end{enumerate}
